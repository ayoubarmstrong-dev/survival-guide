Structural Collapse & TCCC Massive Hemorrhage Doctrine

During urban artillery bombardment and structural earthquakes, blunt trauma, blast shrapnel, and arterial exsanguination account for over 85% of preventable field mortalities.

#### 1. Tactical Combat Casualty Care (TCCC) - MARCH Protocol:

  • M - Massive Hemorrhage Control (Under 60 Seconds):
  • Arterial Limb Bleeding: Immediately apply a genuine Combat Application Tourniquet (CAT Gen 7) directly over bare skin 2 to 3 inches above the wound (or "high and tight" in low-visibility combat environments).
  • Windlass Tightening: Rotate the rigid windlass rod until bright red pulsatile bleeding ceases completely AND distal pulse is palpably absent. Secure the rod within the windlass clip.
  • Mandatory Timestamping: Write the exact time of application directly onto the white writeable time-strap using permanent marker (TIME: HH:MM).
  • Golden Rule: NEVER loosen, release, or intermittently cycle a tourniquet in the field. Safe window is up to 2 hours. Release must be performed exclusively by surgical trauma personnel to prevent fatal reperfusion acidosis, hyperkalemia, and shock.
  • Junctional Hemorrhage (Groin / Armpit / Neck): Tourniquets cannot occlude junctional vessels. Firmly pack the cavity with QuikClot Kaolin Hemostatic Gauze directly against the hemorrhaging vessel. Maintain continuous direct manual pressure for 3 full minutes before applying a pressure bandage.
  • A - Airway Management: Clear rubble and blood; position unconscious casualties in the lateral recovery position.
  • R - Respiration: Seal sucking chest wounds immediately with vented chest seals.
  • C - Circulation: Reassess tourniquets and distal extremity perfusion.
  • H - Hypothermia Prevention: Prevent the lethal trauma triad (hypothermia, coagulopathy, acidosis). Encase the casualty in an aluminized Swiss Safe Mylar Thermal Blanket to conserve metabolic heat immediately, even in warm environments.

    • #### 2. Structural Void Geometry & Rubble Entrapment:

    • Survival Triangles: Lie adjacent to reinforced load-bearing concrete pillars or heavy solid-wood furniture. Avoid door frames and center spans.
    • Dust Mitigation: Filter air through damp cloth to avoid lethal acute silica/pulverized concrete lung trauma.
    • Acoustic Rescue Signaling: Conserve vocal energy; strike metal structural rebar or plumbing pipes with stones in 3 distinct rhythmic pulses every 60 seconds to trigger rescue seismic geophones.
