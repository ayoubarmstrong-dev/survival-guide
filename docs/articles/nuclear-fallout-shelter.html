The First 72 Hours: Nuclear Detonation & Fallout

The primary lethal hazard following a nuclear surface burst (aside from the initial thermal pulse and blast wave) is radioactive fallout particulate descending within 15 minutes to 2 hours downwind.

#### 1. Immediate Reaction to Flash:

  • Never look toward the fireball (causes immediate flash blindness and retinal burns).
  • Drop flat behind terrain or solid concrete, facing away from the detonation, covering your head, and opening your mouth slightly to equalize blast pressure on eardrums.

#### 2. The 7/10 Rule of Radiation Decay:

Radioactive decay follows an exponential logarithmic curve: for every 7-fold increase in time following detonation, the ionizing radiation rate decreases by a factor of 10:

  • 7 Hours post-blast: Radiation drops to 1/10th of its 1-hour rate.
  • 49 Hours (~2 days): Radiation drops to 1/100th (safe for brief critical sorties).
  • 2 Weeks (~336 hours): Radiation drops to 1/1,000th of peak intensity.
  • Core Directive: Remain strictly inside a dense concrete or subterranean basement shelter for 48 to 72 hours minimum before attempting surface movement.
