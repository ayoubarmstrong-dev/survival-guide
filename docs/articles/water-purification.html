Emergency Hydration Standards (WHO & FEMA Protocols)

The human body can survive no longer than 3 days without potable water. The non-negotiable operational baseline is 3.5 to 4.0 liters per person per day (2.0 liters for metabolic hydration, 1.5 to 2.0 liters for medical rehydration and sanitation).

#### 1. Multi-Stage Water Purification Sequence:

  1. Sediment Pre-Filtration: Clarify turbid water by passing it through tightly folded cloth or cotton bandannas to remove suspended debris, silt, and macro-parasites.
  2. 0.1 Micron Hollow-Fiber Microfiltration (Sawyer MINI Protocol):
  3. Forces water through U-shaped micro-tubes with absolute 0.1-micron pores.
  4. Eliminates 99.99999% of pathogenic bacteria (E. coli, Salmonella, Vibrio cholerae) and 99.9999% of protozoan cysts (Giardia lamblia, Cryptosporidium).
  5. Note: Microfilters do not eliminate dissolved heavy metals, chemical agro-toxins, or viral particles (requires secondary disinfection).
  6. Bio-Sand & Hardwood Activated Charcoal Filtration: Layer fine silica sand (10 cm), crushed hardwood activated charcoal (5 cm for pesticide and odor adsorption), and coarse quartz gravel.

#### 2. Chemical Disinfection & Dosages (FEMA & CDC Standards):

  • FEMA Unscented Household Bleach (5%–6% Sodium Hypochlorite):
  • Clear Water: Add 8 drops per gallon (or 2 drops per liter / approx. 0.5 mL).
  • Turbid / Cold Water: Add 16 drops per gallon (or 4 drops per liter / approx. 1.0 mL).
  • Reaction Window: Agitate thoroughly and leave undisturbed for exactly 30 minutes. A faint chlorine odor must be detectable. If absent, repeat the exact dosage and wait an additional 15 minutes.
  • EPA / WHO Sodium Dichloroisocyanurate (NaDCC - Aquatabs 49mg):
  • Dissolve 1 tablet per 1 to 2 liters of clear water.
  • Wait 30 minutes before consumption to achieve a 99.99% log-4 reduction of waterborne viruses and bacteria.
  • Thermal Disinfection (Rolling Boil):
  • Maintain a vigorous rolling boil for 1 full minute at sea level.
  • Maintain for 3 full minutes at elevations exceeding 2,000 meters (6,500 feet).
